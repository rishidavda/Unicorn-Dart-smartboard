# Staff training video — narration script

`winchester-staff-guide.mp4` · 5 min 49 s · 16 scenes.
Regenerate: screenshots via `shots.js`/`cards.js`, narration via Piper (en_GB-alan),
assembly via `build.js` (scene image + voice, concatenated).

| # | Scene | Narration |
|---|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Title card | Welcome to the Winchester darts system. This is the staff guide: the staff console, selling time, the leaderboard, and fixing the usual problems in under a minute. |
| 2 | Diagram card | Each oche is one Windows PC — its own board, TV and players' iPad. One staff iPad runs every board from a single page, and the leaderboard merges them all. Remember the split when something misbehaves: board trouble is Bluetooth or batteries; screen trouble is Wi-Fi or the browser. |
| 3 | Connect page | Turn a PC on and Winchester Darts starts with it. Its screen page shows the address for every view — point a camera at the QR codes. On each TV, tap the page once: that single tap unlocks the announcer for the day. |
| 4 | Staff PIN gate | The staff console lives at slash staff, behind a PIN — one, two, three, four until you change it. Tap it or type it — keyboard and mouse work throughout. One PIN runs every board, and the console locks itself thirty seconds after you walk away. |
| 5 | Staff console (all boards) | One card per board. Take payment, then one hour, two hours, or any number of minutes. Customers cannot start a game until you've put time on the clock, and the countdown starts with their first game — not when you press the button. For pay-at-the-end, press Stopwatch instead: the clock counts up until you press End now. Add fifteen or thirty minutes mid-session, or end a session early. At zero the oche closes itself: the game ends and the names clear. |
| 6 | The till — Played Today & reports | The console is also the till. One venue setting — price per hour, ten pounds unless you change it. Timers charge the time you sold; stopwatches charge time played, minimum an hour, then to the nearest half hour. Every session's bill pops up when it ends, and Played Today lists who played, for how long, and what they owe — with the day's total. At midnight each PC writes the day's PDF report into its reports folder — money taken in big print at the top — and every card links to a today-so-far report any time. |
| 7 | Players iPad, closed | Between sessions the players' iPad shows this. It unlocks the moment you start a timer, and closes again at zero — the next group inherits nothing. |
| 8 | Board & sound fold-out | Under Board and sound on each card: Connect, Wake board — watch the board's rim light turn green — Line up board if every score is wrong by the same rotation, the caller test, and the diagnostics report. And watch the battery pill: a flat board keeps its button working long after darts stop counting. Three double-A cells in the back. |
| 9 | Venue leaderboard (rolling 30 days) | At slash board, the whole venue's leaderboard: a rolling top fifty over the last thirty days — it never empties on the first of the month; old games just drop off the back, and you can restart the table from the console for a new season. Plus the all-time top ten, the record books, and the highest scores: five-oh-one's biggest visits and the furthest triples runs. Read-only, safe on any screen. |
| 10 | Game picker (step 2 of the wizard) | Customers run their own games in two steps: add player names — remembered for the session — then pick from twenty-four games in four groups: classics like five-oh-one and Cricket, party games like Killer, Gotcha and Sudden Death, score races, and practice drills like Bob's twenty-seven. |
| 11 | How to play sheet | Nobody has to ask the bar how a game works: every card has a How to play button with the rules in plain English. Mid-session, the Fix tab restarts the game or takes the same players back to the menu to pick another. |
| 12 | The £1,000 challenge | The Prize tab is the thousand-pound challenge: around the clock, trebles only, twenty-one darts without a single miss. Punters read the rules there — but attempts only count when YOU start them: get the video rolling first, then on that board's card, type their name and press Start attempt. The TV shows the perfect run live; one miss ends it, and a win sets off the full celebration — and a reminder to keep that video safe. |
| 13 | Play screen | During a game, everything fits one screen. Scores at the top, next player, undo dart and miss below, and a picture of the board to tap for bounce-outs — or to play without the smartboard entirely. |
| 14 | TV with 180 | The TV shows every dart as it lands. When a turn ends, it holds the player's three darts and their total for ten seconds, and the announcer calls it out loud — one hundred and eighty included. |
| 15 | Fix-it card | Four fixes solve almost everything. Beeps but no voice: tap that TV's page once. The button works but darts don't count: wake the board from the staff console, then fresh batteries. Scores rotated round the board: line up board, one dart in the twenty. Anything else: the diagnostics report on the board's card. And if the PC was asleep, just wait half a minute — the hub notices the wake-up and reconnects the board by itself. |
| 16 | Outro card | That's the lot. PIN one two three four. Diagnostics dot text if support asks. And for board hardware, Unicorn are on 0115, 985, 3500. Game on! |
